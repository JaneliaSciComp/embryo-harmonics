Horn approximations for the ovoid embryo: $R(z)$ versus $r(\varphi)$

Two 1D reductions of the Laplace–Neumann eigenproblem on the pre-twitch embryo, how they treat the round tips, and how well they reproduce the exact harmonics.

Setting

The pre-twitch embryo is modelled as a body of revolution about its major axis $z$, with outline $$r(\varphi) = \sum_{n=0}^{10} a_n \cos(n\varphi)$$ in polar coordinates around the outline's centroid (the sine terms of the measured series are dropped, which makes the outline mirror symmetric about the axis). We want the Neumann eigenfunctions $-\Delta u = \lambda u$, $\partial_\nu u = 0$. Rotational symmetry separates the angle exactly: $u = v \cos(m\theta)$ or $v\sin(m\theta)$, and the ovoid-symmetric scripts solve for $v$ on the 2D meridian half-plane with finite elements. That solution is the reference for both approximations.

Both horns are Kantorovich reductions: fix the shape of the mode across the body, insert it into the Rayleigh quotient $\int|\nabla u|^2 / \int u^2$, integrate over the transverse coordinate, and minimize over the remaining 1D envelope. They differ in which coordinate is "transverse".

Horn in $z$: radius profile $R(z)$

With $\xi = r/R(z)$, the ansatz takes the Neumann disk mode at every axial station: $$u = A(z)\, g(\xi) \cos(m\theta), \qquad g(\xi) = J_m(j'_{m,l}\,\xi), \quad J_m'(j'_{m,l}) = 0.$$ Integrating over the cross-section ($r\,dr = R^2\xi\,d\xi$) with $G_0 = \int_0^1 g^2\xi\,d\xi$, $G_1 = \int_0^1 g g'\xi^2\,d\xi = \tfrac12 g(1)^2 - G_0$ and $G_2 = \int_0^1 g'^2\xi^3\,d\xi$ gives, per unit length and divided by $G_0$, the energy and mass densities $$R^2 A'^2 \;-\; 2\tfrac{G_1}{G_0} R R' A A' \;+\; \tfrac{G_2}{G_0} R'^2 A^2 \;+\; j'^2_{m,l} A^2, \qquad R^2 A^2 .$$ The plain Webster equation drops the two wall-slope terms, which is justified when $|R'| \ll 1$: $$-(R^2 A')' + j'^2_{m,l} A = \lambda R^2 A .$$ It is exact for a cylinder with flat ends. Every $(m,l)$ branch gives a whole family of envelopes $n = 0, 1, \dots$ (with $n$ nodes along the axis).

The cap problem. At a round tip $R \approx \sqrt{2\rho_c(z - z_0)}$, so $R' \to \infty$: the slow-variation assumption fails exactly where the outline is steepest. Three ways to handle this were tried (--caps):

  • natural: solve the plain equation on the whole axis. The weight $R^2$ vanishes linearly at the tips, which makes them regular singular points: bounded solutions satisfy $R^2A' \to 0$ on their own, and linear finite elements with natural boundary conditions get this right without special treatment.
  • flat: cut the body where the wall gets steeper than 60° to the axis and put flat Neumann ends there. On the ovoid, that removes 1.8 µm and 1.6 µm at the two tips (6% of the length). Beyond the cuts the envelope is continued constantly, with the transverse shape still scaled to the true radius, so the basis covers the whole body.
  • corrected: keep the wall-slope terms, i.e. the full Kantorovich equation. For the $m=0, l=1$ branch $g$ is constant and $G_1 = G_2 = 0$, so it reduces to plain Webster. For the other branches $\int R'^2 A^2\,dz$ diverges logarithmically at a round tip unless $A$ vanishes there; with elementwise constant slopes the discrete weight of this term depends (weakly) on the grid.

Horn in $\varphi$: polar outline $r(\varphi)$

In spherical coordinates $(\rho, \varphi, \theta)$ around the centroid, the body is $\rho < r(\varphi)$. With $\xi = \rho / r(\varphi)$ the ansatz takes, at every polar angle, the radial Neumann mode of a ball of the local radius: $$u = A(\varphi)\, g(\xi) \cos(m\theta), \qquad g(\xi) = j_k(\kappa_{k,p}\,\xi), \quad j_k'(\kappa_{k,p}) = 0,$$ with $j_k$ the spherical Bessel function ($\kappa_{0,1} = 0$, i.e. $g = 1$). The gradient in spherical coordinates is $(\partial_\rho u, \rho^{-1}\partial_\varphi u, (\rho\sin\varphi)^{-1}\partial_\theta u)$ and $\partial_\varphi u = A'g - A g' \xi\, r'/r$. Integrating over $\rho$ with the volume element $\rho^2\sin\varphi\,d\rho$ and $$I_1 = \int_0^1 g'^2\xi^2, \quad I_2 = \int_0^1 g^2, \quad I_3 = \int_0^1 g^2\xi^2, \quad J_1 = \int_0^1 g g'\xi = \tfrac12 g(1)^2 - \tfrac12 I_2$$ gives the energy and mass densities $$\sin\varphi\left[I_2\, r A'^2 - 2 J_1\, r' A A' + I_1\left(r + \frac{r'^2}{r}\right) A^2 + \frac{m^2 I_2\, r}{\sin^2\varphi} A^2\right], \qquad \sin\varphi\; I_3\, r^3 A^2 .$$ Here the slope terms are kept: unlike $R'(z)$, $r'(\varphi)$ is bounded, so they cost nothing.

Exact for a ball. For constant $r$, the Euler–Lagrange equation is the associated Legendre equation, with solutions $P_L^m(\cos\varphi)$ and eigenvalues $(I_1 + L(L+1) I_2)/(I_3 r^2)$. Multiplying the spherical Bessel equation $(\xi^2 g')' + (\kappa^2\xi^2 - k(k+1))g = 0$ by $g$ and integrating by parts (using $g'(1) = 0$) gives $I_1 + k(k+1) I_2 = \kappa_{k,p}^2 I_3$, so the eigenvalue is exactly $(\kappa_{k,p}/r)^2$ for $L = k$, and wrong for every other $L$. Each radial shape $(k, p)$ therefore contributes only one envelope per $m \le k$: the one with $k - m$ nodes, which is $P_k^m$ on the ball.

No cap problem. The tips are $\varphi = 0, \pi$, where only the weight $\sin\varphi$ vanishes, as in the Legendre equation: for $m = 0$ the natural condition is automatic, for $m > 0$ the $m^2/\sin\varphi$ potential forces $A = 0$ (imposed as a Dirichlet condition). $r(\varphi)$ is a smooth Fourier series, so nothing degenerates at the tips.

Midpoint. The expansion point is the centroid of the outline, the origin of the Fourier series. Other choices (midpoint of the extent, best-fitting sphere center) differ by a few voxels here and were not pursued.

The price. The approximation assumes $|r'/r| \ll 1$; on the ovoid $|r'/r|$ reaches 0.67 (at 35° from the axis). Worse, the ansatz ties the number of polar nodes to the order of the radial shape. On a ball that is right, but in an elongated body the axial modes are close to plane waves $\cos(n\pi z/L)$, which a high-order $j_k$ (concentrated near the surface) represents poorly.

The horn in $z$ is clearly better on this geometry: about 2–3% eigenvalue error and 95% of the exact subspace captured even at $N = 300$, against about 10% and 87% for the horn in $\varphi$, whose worst modes (the low axial $m = 0$ modes) are off by up to 60%. The aspect ratio of 1.8 puts the ovoid much closer to a capped cylinder than to a ball. The three cap treatments differ by less than they differ from the exact solution: the tips are only 6% of the length and the vanishing weight $R^2$ keeps the modes out of them. corrected is marginally best for the first 30 modes but biases the higher eigenvalues slightly upwards; natural is the default: unlike flat it needs no cut angle, and unlike corrected it stays accurate over all 300 modes (1.5% against 2.1% mean eigenvalue error) with the simpler equation. The energy of the gene coefficients accumulates almost identically in all bases (not shown in the table), so that metric does not discriminate between them.

Geometry and cell placement

The outline coefficients are given in pixels of 4× upsampled 0.1625 µm images, i.e. 0.040625 µm (--pixel-size). The cells of all 85 pre-twitch time points (20 to 378) are moved into the ovoid by a single translation that minimizes the largest relative radius $|x - c| / r(\varphi)$ over all cells; the major axis is kept along AP. With --flip (first tip of the outline towards negative AP), the outermost cell lies at 96.6% of the outline radius (98.2% without the flip), so no radial extension is needed; --radial-extension is there in case a different pixel size or the cell extent (positions are nuclei) calls for it. The fitted center is stored with the coefficients. All results are in /data/embryo-harmonics/ovoid_20261020-192514.
